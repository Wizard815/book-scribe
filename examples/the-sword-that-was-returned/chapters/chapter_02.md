# Chapter 2: The Boy in the Courtyard

## Scene 1

<!-- paragraph_id: ch2_s1_p1 -->
I first saw him in Sir Ector's courtyard, on a morning when I had ridden in with a message and a sore back. A thin boy of twelve or so was hauling water from the well, two buckets slopping at his sides, while a taller lad leaned on the wall and shouted orders at him. The taller one was Kay, Sir Ector's son, proud of his new spurs, and the boy answered him as a little brother answers, with a grumble and a grin. That was Arthur, though nobody in that yard knew it, least of all Arthur, who believed himself Ector's second son and Kay's to boss about. He saw my horse and forgot his buckets at once, running his hand along its neck and asking its name before he asked mine. I remember thinking he looked at everything as if it might be worth keeping, and that I had never seen a servant's chore done with such gravity.

<!-- paragraph_id: ch2_s1_p2 -->
Before I left that day he caught my sleeve and asked, in a rush, whether I would show him my sword. I should have refused, since it was not my place to arm another man's household, but the pleading in his face was hard to resist. I drew the blade, a plain soldier's sword with a nicked edge, and laid it flat across his two palms. His arms sagged under the weight, and he went red with effort, so I stood behind him and closed his fingers around the grip, moving his wrist until the point rose and held steady. "Thumb along the back," I told him, "and keep your elbow in." Kay, watching from the wall, burst out laughing and called him a scullion playing at knighthood, but Arthur did not so much as glance his way. He kept his eyes on the steel, and when I took it back he looked at his empty hands as if something had been lifted out of them.

<!-- paragraph_id: ch2_s1_p3 -->
At dusk Sir Ector came out onto the steps with a lantern and called the boys in to supper, and I turned to fetch my horse. The lantern light fell across Arthur as he ran past, still holding his hands a little apart, as though the sword's weight lingered in them. Ector did not move or speak, but I saw his face change. It was not anger and not pride; it was an old, patient sorrow, the look of a man watching a loan he has grown fond of and knows he must one day give back. Kay brushed past him and got a pat on the shoulder, while Arthur got only a long, quiet stare. When Ector noticed that I had seen it, he lifted the lantern higher, wished me a safe road, and went inside without another word. I rode out thinking that he knew something about that boy which neither of them had been told.
