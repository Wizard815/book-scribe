# Chapter 1: The Photograph

## Scene 1

<!-- paragraph_id: ch1_s1_p4 -->
![Everett Lund in his uniform, before he sailed](image:everett-photo-c1fc4fb5.jpg)

<!-- paragraph_id: ch1_s1_p1 -->
It began with a photograph, an old one, soft at the edges and gone the color of weak tea. His great-niece turned it over in her hands and found a young man looking back, her great-uncle Everett Lund, long before he was anyone's great-uncle at all. She had known the name all her life, the way a family knows the names of people who were there before it. Now she held his face, and she wanted to know the boy behind it. He came from a wheat farm near Lindsborg, Kansas, the son of Swedish parents, and somewhere between that flat country and the photograph was a story nobody had yet written down. She meant to write it.

<!-- paragraph_id: ch1_s1_p2 -->
In the picture Everett stands in a photographer's studio, in his uniform and his hat, held as stiff as a fence post. He was having it taken before he sailed, and he plainly knew that it mattered. His mouth is pressed flat, and you can see him working at it, trying with all his might not to smile. Whatever he was feeling, the boy from the wheat farm kept it behind his face and let the camera have only the uniform.

<!-- paragraph_id: ch1_s1_p5 -->
Ruth wanted very much to learn who the boy in the photograph had been. The family had kept his name, but the person behind it had slipped away, and she was determined to bring him back. Sitting with the picture in her lap, she tried to imagine the town he came from, and she pictured the church bells of Lindsborg ringing across the wheat on a Sunday morning. She felt sure he had heard them as a child, standing in the yard in his good clothes, long before any uniform. It was a small thing to imagine, but it was where she chose to begin.
